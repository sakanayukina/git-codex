 A B S T R A C T
With the ongoing development of heterogeneous multi-robot systems technology, the challenges related to 
scheduling extended sequences of dynamic and prioritized tasks have become progressively more pronounced. 
To address this issue, this paper proposes an online task scheduling framework for efficient collaboration 
in heterogeneous multi-robot systems. First, a collaboration strategy for heterogeneous multi-robot systems 
is proposed to facilitate efficient parallel work, incorporating online updates to task sequences and priorities. 
Second, a deep reinforcement learning based dynamic task scheduling method employing actor–critic attention 
mechanisms is proposed for online optimal task sequence generation. In addition, task priority is integrated 
into the objective function, encouraging the rapid execution of high-priority tasks. Experimental results with 
randomly generated tasks of varying scales demonstrate the flexibility, efficiency and generalization of the 
method. Task sequences for the heterogeneous robots are generated online as the task set to be executed 
evolves, optimizing the global travel distances while ensuring the timely execution of high-priority tasks.
1. Introduction
As the complexity of heterogeneous multi-robot systems (HMRS) 
is steadily increasing, they show significant potential for application 
in various fields including industry, healthcare, exploration, logistics, 
etc. In these systems, a key challenge lies in effectively performing 
task scheduling and assignment to ensure both efficient operation and 
optimized performance. In fields such as intelligent manufacturing, 
logistics, search and rescue, intelligent inspection, and planetary ex-
ploration, HMRS must adapt to dynamic changes in tasks and priority 
adjustments to achieve efficient and optimized task execution. Task 
scheduling in this context must consider not only the dynamic char-
acteristics of the task sequence, including the length of task sequence 
changes over time, but also must incorporate the priority attributes of 
tasks to optimize and adjust the overall task execution sequence.
In designing task scheduling and assignment strategies, it is essential 
to consider the following key factors in an integrated manner: (1) The 
dynamic nature of tasks: The entire sequence of tasks may change due 
to environmental variations or the addition of new tasks. Therefore, 
the task scheduling mechanism must exhibit flexibility and adaptability 
to handle these unpredictable changes. (2) Task prioritization: In a 
multitasking environment, certain tasks may be assigned higher priority 
based on their urgency or significance. The task allocation system must 
be capable of recognizing these priorities and incorporating them into

2. Related work
2.1. Task scheduling and allocation in multi-robot systems
Task scheduling and allocation, a critical challenge in optimizing 
robotic systems, has become a prominent research focus in recent 
years. In multi-robot systems, task scheduling and allocation involve 
the efficient assignment of tasks to multiple robots to maximize overall 
benefits or achieve specific performance objectives. In recent years, 
multi-robot systems have garnered significant attention due to their 
promising real-world applications. One of the most critical challenges 
in these systems is Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA). This problem 
involves efficiently scheduling and assigning a set of tasks to a group 
of robots to optimize an objective function (e.g., minimizing task 
completion time or distance). The key decision-making challenge lies 
in determining which robot should execute each specific task [2,4].
As the number of tasks and robots grows, developing an effective 
task-scheduling strategy can significantly enhance system efficiency. 
Multi-robot task scheduling involves assigning unfinished tasks to avail-
able robots within specific time and space constraints. Each robot 
performs tasks in the designated order. This requires rapid analysis of 
the task environment, robot capabilities, and task details, as well as 
quick decomposition of the task set and determination of task sequences 
under constraints to maximize benefits and minimize costs. This is an 
NP-hard problem in combinatorial optimization [5,6].
Task allocation for robots and the optimization of a given objective 
function (whether maximization or minimization) is a critical process 
in dynamically changing environments. These environments may be 
influenced by factors such as robot failures, evolving tasks, moving 
obstacles, unknown surroundings, or other unforeseen events, which 
add significant complexity to the problem. As a result, task scheduling 
and allocation in dynamic environments is considered one of the most 
challenging aspects of the problem, requiring decision-making that 
accounts for various constraints that may emerge during task execu-
tion [7,8]. In recent years, the academic community has seen a growing 
body of research focused on dynamic task allocation.
Robot task scheduling and allocation is inherently a combinatorial 
optimization problem. Solving it requires applying optimization tech-
niques to address this combinatorial nature. Some common combina-
torial optimization problems include the Traveling Salesman Problem 
(TSP), the Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP), the Job Shop Scheduling 
Problem, and the Knapsack Problem, among others, which can be 
transformed and extended appropriately to obtain the task scheduling 
and allocation problem model for multi-robot systems [9–11]. For 
example, TSP can be extended to the Multiple Traveling Salesmen 
Problem (mTSP). This problem not only involves task allocation but 
also the optimization of multiple paths, which must visit several task 
locations and return to a common origin [12–14]. By replacing the 
salesmen with robots and the cities with tasks, the mTSP transforms 
into the MRTA problem [15,16].
In some research, the Hungarian algorithm, an operations research 
method known for its high efficiency, was widely used to solve task-
scheduling problems [17]. Other algorithms, such as genetic algo-
rithms, particle swarm optimization, ant colony optimization, were 
also explored [18–20]. So far, traditional methods for solving combi-
natorial optimization problems can be broadly categorized into two 
types: exact approaches and approximate approaches. (1) Exact ap-
proaches are algorithms that can find the global optimal solution 
to a problem, primarily including branch-bound and dynamic pro-
gramming. (2) Approximate approaches are methods that find a local 
optimal solution, primarily including Approximation Algorithms and 
Heuristic Algorithms [21–23]. Exact methods are capable of finding 
the global optimal solution to a combinatorial optimization problem, 
but they consume substantial computational resources as the problem 
size increases, making them difficult to apply to large-scale problems. 
In contrast, approximate methods can find good solutions within an 
acceptable computation time. However, when the problem size is very 
large, even approximate methods require a significant number of iter-
ative searches, leading to a substantial increase in computation time. 
Therefore, approximate methods still face challenges when addressing 
online real-time optimization problems.
2.2. Combinatorial optimization problems with deep reinforcement learning
With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence, DRL has 
achieved significant breakthroughs across various fields, overcoming 
the limitations of traditional approaches. Notably, in solving combina-
torial optimization problems like the TSP, VRP, and knapsack problems, 
DRL-based methods have shown remarkable effectiveness. Compared to 
traditional algorithms, DRL offers advantages such as faster solution 
speeds and better generalization, making it a key focus of current 
research.
A major breakthrough in using neural networks for combinatorial 
optimization occurred in 2015 when Vinyals et al. introduced the 
Pointer Network model [24,25], enabling neural networks to directly 
generate solutions. This opened new research avenues in the field. 
Recently, the rise of graph neural networks (GNNs) has led researchers 
to explore their application in combinatorial optimization. Unlike the 
Pointer Network, GNN-based methods focus on learning node features 
to perform tasks like link and node prediction, facilitating optimization. 
For instance, Dai et al. were the first to combine GNNs with DRL to 
tackle problems like the Minimum Vertex Cover (MVC) and TSP [26–
30].
The methods described above are ‘‘end-to-end’’ approaches, where a 
trained deep neural network directly outputs the solution to a problem 
instance. These methods typically use DRL to train the network param-
eters. Compared to traditional iterative algorithms, end-to-end methods 
eliminate the need for a search process, significantly improving so-
lution speed. Once trained, the model can handle all instances with 
similar distribution characteristics, showcasing strong generalization 
capabilities.
DRL algorithms have achieved local and even global optimal solu-
tions for certain combinatorial optimization problems, with successful 
applications in multi-robot task allocation. However, existing models 
often overlook dynamic task changes and the impact of task priority 
attributes on optimization outcomes. Additionally, research on HMRS 
addressing priority tasks and dynamic changes remains limited [2,3,
31]. This study aims to explore solutions to these critical issues using 
DRL.
This study focuses on optimizing task scheduling in multi-robot 
systems, addressing two key challenges: task sequence planning for het-
erogeneous robots and dynamic scheduling for their collaborative tasks. 
Complex collaborative tasks are decomposed into the classic TSP and its 
extended forms, based on which task scheduling models are developed 
(see details in Section 3. A deep reinforcement learning framework 
incorporating attention mechanisms is proposed to enable a collabo-
rative scheduling system with end-to-end autonomous decision-making 
capabilities Section 4.1.

3.1. Heterogeneous multi-robot collaboration scenario
This section presents a scenario, where a heterogeneous team of 
robots collaboratively travel among a set of target locations and per-
form tasks such as inspection and excavation. This work scenario has 
broad application potential in areas such as intelligent inspection robot 
systems, automatic guided vehicle scheduling, drone patrol missions, 
and cleaning robots, etc.
In a fixed area, multiple stationary targets require inspection, and 
the collaborative work of two heterogeneous robots, as illustrated 
in Fig.  1, demonstrates how the task is performed. This preliminary 
collaboration demonstrates the synergy between the robots, but the 
system is designed to be scalable for larger deployment.
This system includes two types of mobile robots: a reconnaissance 
robot and an excavation robot. These robots collaborate, each pursuing 
its respective objectives, to efficiently complete complex tasks. In this 
scenario, the reconnaissance robot 𝑅1 is responsible for exploring and 
identifying target locations. It first plans the order of visits, then 
evaluates the urgency and importance of tasks at each location based 
on its reconnaissance. The robot determines task priorities and shares 
this information with the excavation robot to guide its excavation 
scheduling. The excavation robot 𝑅2 performs excavation tasks upon 
reaching target locations. It considers both the task priorities shared 
by the reconnaissance robot and the travel distances to determine the 
order of visits, plan, and schedule the excavation tasks accordingly.
To ensure efficient collaboration in the HMRS, the reconnaissance 
robot’s task scheduling must be optimized first. This step aims to 
design the shortest travel path, enabling the robot to complete its 
exploration tasks efficiently. Thus, the scheduling of the reconnaissance 
robot can be modeled as a TSP. The excavation robot, on the other 
hand, must consider both task priorities shared by the reconnaissance 
robot and travel distances, using advanced decision-making algorithms 
to determine the optimal real-time schedule. Hence, the task scheduling 
for the excavation robot can be modeled as a dynamic and prioritized 
TSP.
The two robots collaborate through the following mechanism. Ini-
tially, the reconnaissance robot 𝑅1 executes tasks based on its own 
mission plan. After completing some tasks, 𝑅1 shares task information 
with priority attributes with the excavation robot 𝑅2. The excavation 
robot integrates these tasks, combining those with priority labels from 
𝑅1 and those without, for comprehensive scheduling and planning. 
Tasks not completed by 𝑅1 are assigned low priority, while tasks 
labeled with priority are given higher priority in the schedule. At each

3.3. Key issues
(1) The first step involves decision-making for the task scheduling of 
reconnaissance robot 𝑅1, with an emphasis on optimal path planning. 
This strategy requires 𝑅1 to consider the locations of all known station-
ary targets and determine the globally shortest travel path, ensuring 
efficient completion of the exploration task.
(2) Next, the task scheduling decision for excavation robot 𝑅2 is 
calculated, considering both path and priority in a multi-objective 
optimization. 𝑅2 accounts for the dynamic task sequence, priority 
information from 𝑅1, and task completion distances. A model decision-
making algorithm is used to determine the optimal scheduling. The 
process applies multi-stage planning, with each stage achieving a local 
optimum, which is combined to form a global optimum solution.

4. Optimal task scheduling with deep reinforcement learning
In this section, We propose an Actor–Critic attention mechanism-
based DRL model (AC-Attn-DRL) to address the optimization chal-
lenges described in Section 3. The model utilizes the REINFORCE 
training strategy to optimize performance parameters. Unlike existing 
approaches [25], our method introduces task node priority alongside 
spatial positions in task scheduling. Additionally, we extend beyond

4.3. Greedy search strategy and beam search strategy
In recent years, search strategies have become crucial in deep 
learning, with applications in areas like natural language processing, 
machine translation, and task planning. Effective decoding methods, 
such as the greedy and beam search strategies, are key to solving com-
plex sequential decision-making problems. The greedy strategy selects 
the best option at each step, while beam search considers multiple 
options for a more thorough solution. Both have been extensively 
developed to balance speed and solution quality, making them essential 
for task scheduling and planning in deep learning [33,37–39].
In this study, two distinct search strategies were employed to eval-
uate their effectiveness in solving task scheduling and planning prob-
lems:
(i) Greedy Strategy Decoder: This method selects the node with the 
highest probability as the next target to visit at each decoding step.

A key characteristic of this method is its ability to handle problems 
of varying scales with a model trained on a fixed number of tasks. 
For example, a model trained on 𝑁 = 10 task nodes can generalize to 
tasks with 𝑁 = 5 or 𝑁 = 8, achieving near-optimal solutions when the 
problem differences are small, as confirmed in the literature [25]. This 
capability is applied in this study to implement dynamic scheduling and 
planning for evolving extended-sequence tasks.

Computational results show that, in scenarios with a small number 
of task nodes, the greedy search strategy is generally preferred for its 
speed and simplicity. However, the beam search strategy still demon-
strates superior performance. Even for a small subset of instances, 
the two strategies may yield identical solutions, or the greedy search 
may outperform in a few cases. This suggests that, even in small-scale 
tasks, the beam search method, by exploring the solution space more 
comprehensively, often finds better solutions. As the number of task 
nodes increases and the search space expands, the beam search method 
effectively explores a broader solution space, often finding the optimal 
solution and outperforming the greedy search strategy. Thus, the beam 
search strategy outperforms the greedy search across tasks of various 
scales, with significantly better overall performance. This suggests that, 
particularly for large-scale and complex tasks, the beam search method 
should be prioritized in model design and strategy selection. These find-
ings offer valuable insights for optimizing models in diverse application 
scenarios, particularly for tasks of varying scales. Future research can 
further explore the beam search method’s performance across different 
scales and refine strategies to better meet specific task requirements.
To systematically investigate the effect of the priority weight ad-
justment factor 𝛼 across varying task node counts 𝑁, a detailed experi-
mental design and computational analysis were conducted. The results 
show that 𝛼 significantly impacts the model’s reasoning.
Computational results show that for different task node counts, such 
as 𝑁 = 10, larger values of 𝛼 generally improve performance, whether 
using a greedy or beam search strategy. This is because, in small-scale 
tasks, higher 𝛼 values lead to more focused optimization, significantly 
influencing the search process and the final solution. Similar trends are 
observed for larger values of 𝑁.
Ideally, for a given task instance, once the task node order is fixed, 
there exists an optimal path. However, the comprehensive objective 
function must also account for priority, where a larger 𝛼 increases the 
weight of priority in the function. Conversely, a smaller 𝛼 emphasizes 
minimizing the path length to complete all tasks. As 𝛼 increases, the 
objective function becomes more influenced by priority, showing an 
inverse relationship with the shortest path. Experimental results con-
firm this behavior, validating the rationality of the priority-weighted 
sum index (PWSI) and the sequential priority weight coefficient 𝛽𝜋(𝑖).
As the number of task nodes increases from 𝑁 = 10 to 𝑁 = 
20, computational results show that the influence of 𝛼 becomes less 
pronounced. Although larger values of 𝛼 still affect the outcome, the 
impact is notably reduced compared to 𝑁 = 10. This can be explained 
by the varying total path length as the task nodes increase, which, when 
coupled with a fixed priority weight parameter, diminishes 𝛼’s effect 
on the comprehensive objective function. This trend holds as 𝑁 grows 
from 50 to 100.
Through analyzing 𝛼 under varying total task nodes 𝑁, the follow-
ing conclusions can be drawn: when 𝑁 is fixed, the priority weight 
factor significantly influences the model, regardless of task scale. A 
larger 𝛼 emphasizes priority in task sequencing, while a smaller 𝛼
focuses more on the path. This highlights the need to adapt 𝛼 based 
on task scale and complexity for optimal model performance. These 
findings guide future model optimization, especially for tasks of varying 
scales. Future research can explore the interaction between 𝛼 and task 
scale, as well as more precise adjustments of 𝛼 to meet specific task 
requirements.
The results demonstrate that the multi-objective optimization model 
with priority embedding effectively handles extended-sequence pri-
oritized tasks, fulfilling 𝑅2’s requirement for priority-driven dynamic 
scheduling.

6. Conclusion
This paper proposes a collaborative strategy for heterogeneous 
multi-robot systems. A deep reinforcement learning based task schedul-
ing method is further developed for online task sequence generation, 
accounting for dynamic updates and priority properties of the tasks 
arising from the collaboration. An Actor–Critic attention mechanism-
based deep reinforcement learning model (AC-Attn-DRL model) inte-
grating the priority information is proposed. Two decoding strategies, 
namely greedy search and beam search, are employed. Experimental 
results demonstrate that the method can generate task sequences online 
in a flexible and adaptive manner as the task set to be executed evolves. 
Furthermore, the method shows good generalization across different 
task scales and the effectiveness of considering task priorities. This 
approach enhances the efficiency and flexibility of collaboration in 
heterogeneous multi-robot systems. In the future, we would like to 
further optimize key parameters and refine collaboration strategies, 
with the aim of substantially improving global efficiency.
